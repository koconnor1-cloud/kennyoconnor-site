Professional
Communication Toolkit

Clear communication is a business skill. Make it easy for other people to understand what you need, what you recommend, and what happens next.

Write emails people can act on

Professional email should reduce friction for the reader.

  • Use a specific subject line.
  • State the purpose early.
  • Give only the context needed to make the decision.
  • Make the requested action and deadline explicit.
  • Proofread names, dates, attachments, and tone before sending.

Show up prepared for meetings

Preparation changes how others experience your professionalism.

  • Know the purpose of the meeting.
  • Bring the information you may be asked to provide.
  • Take useful notes and capture decisions.
  • Clarify ownership and next steps before the meeting ends.

Present for the audience

A presentation is not a written report on slides. Organize around the decision or understanding the audience needs.

  • Lead with the main point.
  • Use evidence to support—not bury—the recommendation.
  • Make charts and visuals readable quickly.
  • Practice transitions and timing aloud.

Close the loop

Reliability is built by follow-through.

  • Confirm important commitments in writing.
  • Do what you said you would do.
  • Communicate early if a deadline is at risk.
  • Follow up with a concise status or outcome.
