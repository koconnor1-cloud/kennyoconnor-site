Think in networks

A flight is not an isolated product. Schedule, aircraft, crews, gates, maintenance, passenger connections, and downstream rotations are linked.

  • Trace how one delay can propagate across the network.
  • Identify scarce resources such as gates, crews, and aircraft.
  • Distinguish local optimization from network performance.
  • Consider recovery options and their downstream effects.

Connect operations to economics

Airlines balance demand, capacity, frequency, price, service, and cost under intense competitive and regulatory constraints.

  • Separate fixed, variable, and opportunity costs.
  • Consider load factor together with yield and revenue.
  • Ask how network structure changes economics.
  • Look for tradeoffs between utilization and resilience.

Use simulation to learn from decisions

A simulation is useful when you explain why a decision produced an outcome—not only whether your team won.

  • Record the assumptions behind each decision.
  • Compare expected and actual results.
  • Identify second-order effects and competitor responses.
  • Use each round to improve the next decision.

Explore the industry broadly

Aviation careers extend far beyond pilots and flight attendants.

  • Airport operations and administration.
  • Airline revenue, network planning, and operations.
  • Cargo, logistics, and ground services.
  • Technology, safety, regulation, customer experience, and consulting.
